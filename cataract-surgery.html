Cataract Surgery Overview

A highly common and generally safe procedure to restore vision by replacing a cloudy lens with a clear artificial one.

Restoring Visual Clarity

Cataract surgery is a standard procedure performed by an eye doctor (ophthalmologist) to remove the lens of your eye when it becomes cloudy and replace it with an artificial lens.

The procedure is performed on an outpatient basis, meaning you don't have to stay in the hospital after the surgery. While cataracts can eventually affect vision, surgery is a safe and reliable solution to regain clarity.

Having a cataract can be like looking through a foggy or dusty car windshield. Surgery clears this "haze" by replacing the natural lens with an Intraocular Lens (IOL).

When to Consider Surgery

Cataracts can cause blurry vision and significantly increase glare from lights. If a cataract makes it difficult for you to carry out your usual activities, your health care team may suggest surgery.

In many cases, waiting to have cataract surgery won't harm your eye. If your vision is still quite good, you may not need surgery for many years, if ever.

Clinical Necessity

Cataract surgery may be essential if the cloudiness interferes with treating other eye problems. For example, if it prevents your doctor from monitoring conditions like Macular Degeneration or Diabetic Retinopathy.

Self-Assessment Checklist

Keep these questions in mind when deciding if it's time for intervention:

  • Can you see to safely do your job and to drive?
  • Do you have problems reading or watching television?
  • Is it difficult to cook, shop, or take medicines?
  • Do bright lights make it more difficult to see?
  • Does vision impact your level of independence?

The Surgical Procedure: Phacoemulsification

1

Preparation & Numbing

Your eye is numbed with drops or an injection. While you remain awake, you may see light and movement but will not see the procedure itself.

2

Tiny Incisions

The surgeon creates miniature incisions (using a blade or laser) near the corneal edge to reach the cataract.

3

Phacoemulsification

An ultrasound instrument breaks up the center of the cloudy lens and suctions it out with high precision.

4

IOL Placement

The new artificial IOL is put into place. Most incisions are self-sealing and rarely require stitches.

Before & Day of Surgery

  • Measurement: Your surgeon will measure your eye to determine the proper focusing power for your IOL.
  • Medication: You may be prescribed antibiotic or anti-inflammatory eye drops to start before the procedure.
  • Fasting: You may be asked not to eat solid food for 6 hours before surgery.
  • Outpatient: The procedure is done on an outpatient basis; you will rest in recovery for 15-30 minutes before going home.

Cataract Recovery Care

  • Avoid Irritants: Do not get soap or water directly in the eye for the first week.
  • No Pressure: Do not rub or press on your eye. Wear your protective shield while sleeping.
  • Eye Drops: Strictly follow your doctor’s schedule for post-operative drops to prevent infection.
  • Activity: Your surgeon will advise when it is safe to resume driving, exercise, and heavy lifting.

Understanding the Risks

While cataract surgery is one of the most common and safest procedures, potential risks include:

Infection & Bleeding

Rare risks that are mitigated through pre and post-op medicated eye drops.

Retinal Swelling/Detachment

Swelling of the retina or the retina lifting from the back of the eye may require further treatment.

IOL Dislocation

The artificial lens implant may shift out of its intended position over time.

Vision Anomalies

Seeing halos, glare, or dark shadows, which often resolve as the eye heals.

*Note: Cataract surgery will not restore vision loss due to separate conditions like Macular Degeneration or Glaucoma.

Posterior Capsular Opacification (PCO)

It is common for vision to become cloudy again weeks, months, or years after surgery. This is often called a "Secondary Cataract." This happens when the membrane (posterior capsule) holding the IOL becomes cloudy.

This is easily corrected with a simple, painless laser procedure called a YAG Laser Capsulotomy, which clears the membrane to restore sharp vision.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main types?

The two common types are Phacoemulsification (Phaco), which uses ultrasound, and Extracapsular Extraction (ECCE), which uses a larger incision.

What is laser cataract surgery?

Surgeons use advanced lasers to make precise incisions and fragment the lens, often resulting in less recovery time than traditional techniques.

Are there benefits to laser?

Yes—increased accuracy and faster recovery. It is specifically advised for complex cataracts or those with high astigmatism.

Is cataract surgery safe?

It is one of the safest and most successful surgeries globally, with most patients enjoying significantly improved vision.

How do I choose?

Your ophthalmologist will help you choose based on your eye health, lifestyle, and visual goals.

What about the risks?

Rare risks include infection or glare. These are unusual and highly treatable with appropriate post-operative care.
