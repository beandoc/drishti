Squint (Strabismus)

Understanding misalignment of the eyes and why early intervention is critical for healthy vision development.

What is Squint?

Normally, our eyes are perfectly aligned and point in the same direction. "Squint" or Strabismus describes eyes that are not properly aligned, with one eye being straight and the other turned away from the straight-ahead position.

It may be present all the time (constant) or seen only at times (intermittent), such as when a child is tired, reading, looking at a distance, or exposed to bright light.

Developmental Milestone

At birth, an infant's eyes move independently. By 3 to 4 months of age, eyes should have developed the ability to focus on small objects and appear straight. Any squinting beyond this age is not normal and requires immediate attention.

Signs & Symptoms

*Note: A child with strabismus usually has no symptoms; parents are often the first to notice the misalignment.

  • Misalignment: Eyes crossing or wandering outwards.
  • Light Sensitivity: Squinting or closing one eye in bright light.
  • Head Posture: Tilting the head or turning the face sideways while watching TV.
  • Red Reflex: Abnormality where only one eye shows a red reflex in photographs.
  • Vision Impact: Noticeable decrease in vision or Sudden Double Vision (Adults).

Critical: Diagnostic correction must occur before age 5-6 to preserve Stereopsis (3-D vision).

Differential Causes

The movement of the eyes is controlled by six extraocular muscles. Imbalance in these muscles or their supply nerves leads to squint:

  • In Children: Spontaneous development, refractive errors, or conditions like cataracts and retinal diseases.
  • In Adults: Often results from Diabetes, Hypertension, Hypercholesterolemia, Myasthenia, or injury.

False Squint (Pseudo-strabismus)

Certain children, particularly Asian babies, may appear to have a squint due to a broad flat nose or extra skin folds (inner canthus). This disappears as the face grows and requires only reassurance, not treatment.

Clinical Evaluation

Our dedicated team of specialists conducts a thorough evaluation using advanced diagnostic tests:

Ocular Mapping

Detailed Spectacle power, Slit lamp, and Retina examinations to rule out underlying eye diseases.

Muscle & Perception

Measurement of squint degree, Stereovision tests, HESS charting, and Diplopia charting.

specialized Trials

Prism trials, Orthoptic evaluation, and tests for suppression to map how the brain processes images.

Neuro-Ophthalmic

Consultations with neurologists or brain imaging (MRI/CT) if a neurological root cause is suspected.

Stepwise Treatment Pathway

The main goal is to restore vision first, then straighten the eyes to attempt binocularity (3-D vision).

Step 1: Treat Amblyopia

Focus on restoring vision in the lazy eye. This is done by patching the good eye or blurring its vision using atropine eye drops or high plus powered glasses.

Step 2: Correct Alignment

Once vision in the lazy eye is restored to normal, alignment is corrected. Earlier intervention leads to better results in developing 3-D vision (Stereopsis).

Non-Surgical options

In selective cases, squint may be amenable to treatment with specialized glasses or orthoptic exercises alone.
